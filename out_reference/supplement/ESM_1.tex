\section*{Table S2: Systematic test of scene effects on pose quality}

Each attribute is tested inside one device and one recording, so the day, the wearer, the time of day and the illumination are held fixed. Only windows with functioning tracking and a walking speed between 0.8 and 1.6~m\,s$^{-1}$ enter. For a continuous descriptor the association with the positional indicator is the Spearman correlation with the walking speed partialled out; for a binary annotation it is the ratio of the median indicator between the two sides, which are additionally required to have median speeds within $0.15$~m\,s$^{-1}$ of each other. The direction is then tested across the recordings with a two-sided sign test. An attribute with fewer than 6 usable recordings cannot reach $p < 0.05$ by that test at all and is marked as not testable; counting such attributes as null results would make the negative finding look stronger than the design supports.

Of the 141 attributes listed, 63 are testable and 4 reach the $5$~\% level, against 3.2 expected by chance.

\section*{Table S5: Sensitivity to the high-pass cut-off}

The positional indicator is defined as the root mean square after a high-pass filter, and the manuscript uses a cut-off of $5$~Hz. The table shows what happens at other cut-offs. The absolute values fall as the cut-off rises, as they must, but the ordering of the devices is identical at every cut-off tested, so it is not a property of the chosen frequency. The walking-to-standing ratio does not fall as the cut-off rises either: were the increase under walking residual gait leaking through the filter, raising the cut-off above the gait band would remove it and the ratio would collapse towards unity.
